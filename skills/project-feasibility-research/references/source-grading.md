# Source Grading v0.1

Grade the specific source, not the reputation of a nearby organization.

| Grade | Meaning | Typical use | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Primary | Owner of the product/rule/data publishes the evidence | platform capability, official limit, law, standard, filing, official price | may omit operational failure or commercial downside |
| B Authoritative Secondary | Credible independent synthesis with transparent method/sources | comparison, research result, expert interpretation | still an interpretation of primary material |
| C Market | Vendor, analyst, marketplace, or pricing material | option discovery, quoted commercial position | commercial bias and incomplete comparison |
| D Community | Practitioner report, forum, issue, review, implementation story | real failure modes, usability and integration friction | anecdotal, version/context dependent |
| E Inference | Analyst synthesis with no external source | connect evidence, state bounded hypothesis | must never masquerade as a source |

Rules:

- A source gets Grade A only when it is first-party for the claim being made.
- Every A-D item needs a direct URL, publisher, access date, finding, and
  limitations.
- Never cite a search-result page as evidence when the underlying source is
  available.
- Marketing claims remain Grade C even when published on a vendor's website,
  unless the claim is a directly verifiable official specification or price.
- Community evidence can expose a failure mode but cannot alone prove universal
  infeasibility.
- Contradictory sources remain separate evidence items; do not average them into
  false certainty.
